Convergence criteria (structsolve.convergence)#

Convergence criteria of the non-linear solvers

The equilibrium iterations of Analysis stop when the residual force vector is small, measured by one of two criteria selected with Analysis.convergence:

  • 'crisfield', the default: Crisfield’s criterion, the residual scaled with the diagonal of the linear stiffness matrix and compared with the largest scaled norm of the reference force vectors, the external and the internal ones and, when they apply, the external force vector of the increment of the arc-length solvers and the load factor times \(\partial R/\partial \lambda\), over the free degrees-of-freedom only, see crisfield_zeta();

  • 'relative': the Euclidean norm of the residual compared with the largest Euclidean norm of the force vectors, Analysis.relTOL, or the largest absolute residual force, Analysis.absTOL.

Notes#

The need for scaling

In beam, plate and shell models the degrees-of-freedom mix translations and rotations, such that the residual mixes forces and moments, and its Euclidean norm \(\|R\|\) adds the squares of forces and of moments: the quantity has no physical meaning and its value depends on the system of units, the moments being scaled by a change of the length unit while the forces are not [1]. Each component is therefore scaled with the corresponding diagonal entry of a reference stiffness matrix, an idea of [2] that [3] (Eqs. 27 and 28) used in the convergence test of his arc-length procedure, with the diagonal of the tangent stiffness matrix at the beginning of the increment, and that [1] recommends for convergence testing:

\[d_j = K_{{C0}_{jj}}, \qquad \|v\|_{\boldsymbol{d}} = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{v_j^2}{|d_j|}}\]

with \(n\) the number of free degrees-of-freedom and \(d_j\) the entries of the diagonal scaling vector \(\boldsymbol{d}\). \(d_j\) is a stiffness, of the units of the generalized force of the degree-of-freedom \(j\) divided by those of its generalized displacement, such that each term \(v_j^2/|d_j|\) has the units of energy, whether the degree-of-freedom is a translation or a rotation: \(\|v\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) is the square root of an energy-like quantity, independent of the units of each degree-of-freedom. The absolute value guards against negative diagonal entries.

Crisfield’s criterion

The iteration \(i\) has converged when

\[\zeta_i = \frac{\|R_i\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}}{\max\left( \|\lambda F_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|F_{int}(u_i)\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\Delta\lambda F_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|\lambda \, \partial R/\partial \lambda\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\right)} \le \epsilon\]

with \(\epsilon\) = Analysis.relTOL, \(\lambda\) the current load factor and \(\{F_{ext}\}\) the reference external force vector. The third reference only applies to the arc-length solvers, \(\Delta\lambda\) being the increment of the load factor of the current step, and the fourth one only to load vectors that depend on the load factor, e.g. prescribed displacements imposed exactly, see callbacks. These are the references of the relative criterion, convergence='relative', measured here with the scaled norm. The maximum does not degenerate: under self-equilibrated loads \(\|\lambda F_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) may be negligible whereas \(\|F_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) reflects the internal force carried by the structure; at the beginning of a load-controlled analysis the internal forces are small and the external load is the reference; when the arc-length solvers pass through a load factor close to zero, \(\Delta\lambda F_{ext}\) is the reference; and under prescribed displacements alone the external and the free part of the internal force vectors both vanish at equilibrium, whereas \(\lambda \, \partial R/\partial \lambda\), the out-of-balance force that the prescribed displacements produce with the free degrees-of-freedom held fixed, does not. The norms are evaluated over the free degrees-of-freedom only, since the components of the residual at the constrained ones are the support reactions, not an imbalance. \(\boldsymbol{d}\) is computed once from the linear stiffness matrix calc_kC(), assembled once for the whole analysis, and kept constant during the iterations, such that \(\zeta_i\) measures the progress of the iterations and not the evolution of the tangent stiffness matrix. The internal force vector of the numerator and of the denominator comes from the same evaluation, at no additional cost.

With a consistent tangent stiffness matrix the Newton-Raphson iterations converge quadratically, \(\zeta_{i+1} \sim \zeta_i^2\), and a tight tolerance such as \(\epsilon = 10^{-9}\) is reached in a few iterations; with the modified Newton-Raphson method the convergence is linear. A number of iterations consistently larger than three or four for moderate increments indicates a tangent stiffness matrix inconsistent with the internal force vector, which is why \(\zeta_i\) is printed at every iteration.

The criterion applies to the arc-length methods [3], which only change how the load factor is updated, not the residual, with the additional reference \(\Delta\lambda F_{ext}\).

The relative criterion

With Analysis.convergence = 'relative', the iteration has converged when \(\|R\| \le\) relTOL \(\max(\|F_{ext}\|, \|F_{int}\|, ...)\), with the Euclidean norm over all the degrees-of-freedom of the solver, or when \(\max|R_j| \le\) absTOL, which depends on the units of the model, see check_convergence().

Names of the convergence criteria accepted by Analysis.convergence

structsolve.convergence.check_convergence(an, R, fint, fext_total, fext_ref=None, q_ref=None, w=None)#

Check the convergence of the residual force vector

With an.convergence = 'crisfield' the convergence is achieved when \(\zeta \le\) an.relTOL, see crisfield_zeta(), with the weights w. With an.convergence = 'relative' it is achieved when either:

  • norm(R) <= an.relTOL*max(norm(fext_total), norm(fint), norm(fext_ref), norm(q_ref)), with the Euclidean norm, if an.relTOL is not None;

  • max(abs(R)) <= an.absTOL, if an.absTOL is not None.

an.absTOL is also accepted as an additional criterion with 'crisfield'.

Parameters:
anAnalysis

Analysis object with the attributes convergence, relTOL and absTOL.

Rnp.ndarray

Residual force vector, 1-D array.

fintnp.ndarray

Internal force vector of the same evaluation, 1-D array.

fext_totalnp.ndarray

External force vector at the current load factor, 1-D array.

fext_refnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference of both criteria, the external force vector of the current increment of the arc-length solvers. Default None.

q_refnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference of both criteria, the load factor times \(\partial R/\partial \lambda\) when calc_fext accepts lbd, see callbacks. Default None.

wnp.ndarray or None, optional

Weights of crisfield_weights(), required with 'crisfield'. Default None.

Returns:
convergedbool

Whether the criterion is satisfied.

Rnormfloat

Norm of the residual used by the criterion, \(\|R\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) with 'crisfield', the Euclidean norm with 'relative', used by the divergence checks of the solvers.

zetafloat

Relative residual, \(\zeta\) with 'crisfield', norm(R) over the largest Euclidean norm of the references with 'relative'.

Raises:
ValueError

When an.convergence is not one of CRITERIA, when an.relTOL and an.absTOL are both None, or when w is missing with 'crisfield'.

Notes

The relative criterion is the only one of structsolve up to version 0.8.0, with the default relTOL=1e-6; its reference fext_ref is useful when the load factor is close to zero, where fext_total and fint are small. When all its references are null, the relative residual is norm(R) itself.

structsolve.convergence.crisfield_weights(K0)#

Weights \(1/|d_j|\) of the scaled norm of Crisfield’s criterion

The weight of each free degree-of-freedom is the inverse of the modulus of the diagonal entry of K0. The free degrees-of-freedom are those whose row or column of K0 is not null, as the ones kept by the solvers, see sparseutils.remove_null_cols(); the others get a null weight, i.e. they do not enter the norms.

Parameters:
K0sparse_matrix or np.ndarray

Linear stiffness matrix, shape (n, n), normally calc_kC().

Returns:
wnp.ndarray

Weights, 1-D array of size n, of the inverse of the dimension of the diagonal entries of K0.

Raises:
ValueError

When the diagonal entry of a free degree-of-freedom is null, which leaves its scaling undefined.

See also

crisfield_zeta

the criterion that uses them.

Notes

\(w_j = 1/|K_{{C0}_{jj}}|\) at the free degrees-of-freedom, see the module documentation, and \(w_j = 0\) at the degrees-of-freedom whose row and column of \([K_{C0}]\) are both null, i.e. constrained by zeroing them.

structsolve.convergence.crisfield_zeta(R, fext, fint, w, q=None, fext_inc=None)#

Relative force imbalance \(\zeta\) of Crisfield’s criterion

Parameters:
Rnp.ndarray

Residual force vector, fext - fint, 1-D array of size n.

fextnp.ndarray

External force vector at the current load factor, 1-D array of size n.

fintnp.ndarray

Internal force vector, from the same evaluation as R, 1-D array of size n.

wnp.ndarray

Weights of crisfield_weights(), 1-D array of size n.

qnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference, the load factor times \(\partial R/\partial \lambda\) when the load vector depends on the load factor, see callbacks. Default None.

fext_incnp.ndarray or None, optional

Additional reference, the external force vector of the increment of the arc-length solvers, \(\Delta\lambda \{F_{ext}\}\). Default None.

Returns:
zetafloat

\(\|R\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) over the largest scaled norm of the references, dimensionless, 0. when the residual and all the references are null.

Notes

The criterion is \(\zeta \le \epsilon\), see the module documentation: the reference is \(\max(\|F_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}, \|F_{int}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}})\), with also \(\|q\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) and \(\|\Delta\lambda F_{ext}\|_{\boldsymbol{d}}\) when given. When the reference is null and the residual is not, \(\zeta\) is infinite.
