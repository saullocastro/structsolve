Linear solvers of the static analyses#

Every iteration of a non-linear analysis of Analysis solves a linear system with the tangent stiffness matrix, and the linear analysis does so with the linear stiffness matrix when Analysis.constraints is given. The factorization is chosen with Analysis.linear_solver, see static.LINEAR_SOLVERS and static._factorize(), whose Notes give the algorithms. All of them remove the null rows and columns first, see Boundary conditions and vector sizes, and border the matrix with the constraints of Analysis.constraints.

The choice changes the speed, not the result: the solutions agree to the round-off, such that an analysis takes the same increments and Newton-Raphson iterations with any of them, unless a matrix is so ill-conditioned that the round-off matters.

Choosing a solver for speed#

  • 'splu' needs nothing beyond SciPy and works everywhere, also in the browser (Running in the browser (Pyodide)). SuperLU recomputes its ordering at every factorization and runs on one thread.

  • 'pardiso' is the fastest for large symmetric tangents, e.g. the geometrically non-linear Ritz models of plates and shells under conservative loads, and it also gives the inertia.

  • 'pardiso_lu' is the fastest for large unsymmetric tangents, or symmetric ones whose inertia is not needed, e.g. the tangents of cohesive-zone models of delamination: PARDISO runs on several threads and keeps the analysis (ordering and symbolic factorization) of the sparsity pattern along the analysis, such that each new factorization is only numerical.

  • 'dense_lu' is the fastest for small matrices, and for matrices so dense that a sparse factorization gains nothing, e.g. the Ritz models with global functions, whose tangent has most of its terms; its cost grows with the cube of the size, whatever the sparsity, and it needs the memory of the dense matrix.

'pardiso' and 'pardiso_lu' require the shared library mkl_rt of Intel oneMKL, see pardiso; without it they raise an ImportError (pip install mkl or structsolve[pardiso]). The eigenvalue analyses lb() and freq() accept only 'splu' and 'pardiso', see lb().

Accuracy checks and fallback of 'pardiso_lu'#

PARDISO pivots only within blocks of the matrix and perturbs the pivots that are too small, which keeps the factorization fast and parallel but may make it inaccurate for an ill-conditioned matrix. Hence 'pardiso_lu' checks the relative residual of every solution against static.PARDISO_LU_RTOL; a factorization with perturbed pivots that fails the check is made again with the parameters static.PARDISO_LU_FALLBACK_IPARM, and when that fails as well, or when a later solution fails the check, SuperLU factorizes the matrix and its solution is used. Each fallback to SuperLU issues a RuntimeWarning and a log message; along an analysis, the next factorizations of the same sparsity pattern go to the fallback directly. The analysis is then correct, at the speed of 'splu' or a little slower, see static._factorize().

The accuracy of PARDISO depends on the version of oneMKL: on the tangent of a cohesive-zone model, oneMKL 2025.0 perturbed tens of pivots and gave wrong solutions with its default parameters, where oneMKL 2023.1 was accurate, and the fallback parameters recovered solutions as accurate as those of SuperLU. When the warnings of the fallback to SuperLU appear at every iteration, 'splu' or 'dense_lu' is faster than 'pardiso_lu'.
